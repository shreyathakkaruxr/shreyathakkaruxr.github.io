Designing around home cleaning routines

In-home studies that guided product priorities across the cleaning range.

Overview Context Emerging behavior Key insight Strategic bet Impact

Overview

How can cleaning behaviors inform product design and reveal new user segments? I led ethnography in 15 households and a survey of 500+ people across 4 regions.

Role
Lead Ethnographic Researcher
Timeline
3 weeks
Teams
Global UX (3 researchers), Product Development, Innovation Strategy

Context

The team understood general cleaning tasks well. They knew less about why and how people clean in different homes and regions.

  • EthnographyIn-home visits with 15 households
  • Surveyn = 500+, across Europe, Asia, LATAM, and NA
  • Emotion mappingOpen-ended responses on how cleaning feels

Emerging behavior

Cleaning needs stack in three layers.

  • ControlPeople control, robots execute. Participants ran robots when home, picked rooms, and overrode preset schedules.
  • RitualCleaning is part of the daily routine: while cooking, with a TV or podcast on, and before guests arrive.
  • HealthHouseholds managing a medical condition cleaned differently and chose different products.

Key insight

Households managing a medical condition are a distinct, underserved segment.

Team believed
Design vacuums for general cleaning tasks.
Research showed
Medical needs shaped how often people cleaned, what they cleaned, and what they bought. 52% of participants with a medical condition owned an air purifier.

Strategic bet

Design for health-driven cleaning rituals and medical needs.

Recommended
Use the Control → Ritual → Health needs framework to set product priorities, with health at the top.
What changed
A health-focused innovation direction, and input to the 3-year roadmap.

Impact to date

  • $10MStrategic brand investment in a health-focused direction
  • 1New user segment: households managing a medical condition
  • 12Actionable insights for product and innovation teams
  • 3-yearRoadmap informed by the needs framework
