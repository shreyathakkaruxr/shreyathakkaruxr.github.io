Designing around cleaning routines

In-home studies that guided product priorities across the cleaning range.

Overview

Home cleaning across 4 regions: how people clean different surfaces, their routines, habits and motivations. 15 households and 500+ survey responses.

Role
Lead Ethnographic Researcher
Timeline
3 weeks
Teams
Global UX (3 researchers), Product Development, Innovation Strategy

Context

The team understood general cleaning tasks well. They knew less about why and how people clean in different homes and regions.

Key discoveries

How people clean, their routines, and the surfaces they care for fit into three layers. Each one shapes the others.

Insights shown are from the North America ethnography. All participants managed allergies or other health conditions.

01 · Control

People decide when the robot vacuum runs.

“Sometimes we skipped some rooms because if the child’s room is too messy… it’s going to be a disaster.”

People ran robot vacuums when home, chose rooms, and kept up care like emptying bins and washing pads.

02 · Ritual

People clean a little, often.

“Each bathroom has cleaning stuff in it so I don’t have to carry supplies between rooms.”

People cleaned in small moments, while cooking, before guests, and more when someone was sick.

Supporting findings

Hacks and workarounds

People adapted products and spaces to save effort.

e.g.

  • A plastic board behind the stove to stop splashes
  • Cleaning supplies kept in every bathroom

Surfaces and hard-to-reach spots

Hard-to-reach spots caused the most frustration.

e.g.

  • Appliance interiors
  • Tall windows, fans, and blinds

Vacuum personas

Two approaches to vacuuming emerged.

e.g.

  • Allergy Defender: wants sealed bags
  • Visual Inspector: wants to see the dirt

In their words

“I can handle the mess but not the dirty mess.”

Snippets from the report. Details withheld.

03 · Health

Health needs shape how people clean.

“I’m definitely more particular about cleaning now… I clean differently and focus more on sanitizing.”

Assumption
Design vacuums for general cleaning tasks.
Evidence
Allergies and other health needs shaped how often people cleaned and what they bought. 52% with a medical condition owned an air purifier (survey, n = 500+).
Impact
A health-focused innovation direction, and input to the 3-year roadmap.
