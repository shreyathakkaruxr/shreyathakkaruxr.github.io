Designing around home cleaning routines

In-home studies that guided product priorities across the cleaning range.

Overview Context Key discoveries 01 Control 02 Ritual 03 Health Impact Research approach

Overview

How can cleaning behaviors inform product design and reveal new user segments? I led ethnography in 15 households and a survey of 500+ people across 4 regions.

Role
Lead Ethnographic Researcher
Timeline
3 weeks
Teams
Global UX (3 researchers), Product Development, Innovation Strategy

Context

The team understood general cleaning tasks well. They knew less about why and how people clean in different homes and regions.

Key discoveries

Home care works in three layers, and each one shapes the others.

01 · Control

People control, robots execute.

People ran robots when they were home, picked which rooms to clean, and adjusted settings by area. They also kept up regular care: emptying bins, cleaning filters, and washing mop pads.

02 · Ritual

Cleaning is part of the daily routine.

People cleaned in small steps through the day: while cooking, after the morning routine, and before guests, often with a TV or podcast on. Cleaning increased during illness.

03 · Health

Households managing a medical condition are a distinct, underserved segment.

Team believed
Design vacuums for general cleaning tasks.
Research showed
Allergies and other health needs shaped how often people cleaned, what they cleaned, and what they bought. 52% of participants with a medical condition owned an air purifier.
Recommended
Design for health-driven cleaning rituals and medical needs, with health at the top of the needs framework.
What changed
A health-focused innovation direction, backed by a $10M strategic investment, and input to the 3-year roadmap.
