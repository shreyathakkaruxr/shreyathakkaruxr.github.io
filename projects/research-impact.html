How findings move from one study to teams, decisions, and products.

Approach

Four steps to record impact after each study.

Scroll for more →

1

Send a follow-up

4–6 weeks after each study, to the PM, design, and engineering leads

  1. What changed after the study?
  2. Which metric are you tracking?
  3. Who else should I contact?
  4. Can I share your quote?

2

Update the log

Every Friday · 10 min

  1. PlanSet the decision, metric, and baseline at kickoff.
  2. SignalsRecord who attended and where findings were cited.
  3. Impact logRecord who used the research, what they did, and what changed.
  4. ConfirmationAsk stakeholders to confirm what changed and why.

3

Tag each entry by horizon

From early signals to measured outcomes

    Impact horizons in detail

    Each log entry gets one horizon.

    Select a segment or a horizon.

    Signal

    Teams are engaging with the research

    Evidence
    Sessions joined. Stakeholders attended sessions, readouts, or field visits
    Findings quoted. Findings appear in decks, specs, roadmaps, or leadership updates
    When it shows up
    During the study and in the first weeks after

    Decision

    The research informed a decision

    Evidence
    Decision confirmed. A stakeholder confirms the study informed a specific decision
    Risk avoided. A feature, launch, or investment was changed, paused, or stopped
    When it shows up
    Within weeks to a few months

    Change

    A product or process changed

    Evidence
    Product change. A feature, flow, hardware, or content change
    Ways of working. A team adopted new criteria, rituals, or methods
    When it shows up
    Within one to two quarters

    Outcome

    A metric moved against the baseline

    Evidence
    Metric moved. The kickoff metric changed, such as satisfaction, task success, returns, or support calls
    Cost or time saved. Rework, support load, or development time decreased
    When it shows up
    After launch, often two or more quarters later
